IMPORTANT: Veterinary care comes first

This resource provides educational first-aid information only. It does not replace veterinary diagnosis, treatment or professional advice.

If an animal is struggling to breathe, unconscious, collapsing, bleeding severely, having repeated seizures or experiencing another life-threatening emergency, contact an emergency veterinarian immediately.

Do not delay transport or professional care to complete this guide. If veterinary services are far away, telephone the nearest veterinary hospital for instructions while arranging transport.

🌾 No veterinarian nearby?

Living in a rural or remote community can make accessing emergency veterinary care difficult.

  1. Call the nearest veterinary hospital, even if it is far away.
  2. Explain the animal's symptoms and when they began.
  3. Follow professional instructions while arranging safe transportation.
  4. Prepare the animal's age, approximate weight, medications and possible exposure history.
  5. Do not give human medications or attempt invasive procedures without veterinary direction.

Online information cannot replace a veterinary assessment.

☎️ Need help navigating a veterinary emergency?

Animal HealthLink

Animal HealthLink provides veterinary telephone triage services through participating veterinary practices, using experienced Registered Veterinary Technologists (RVTs).

RVTs use veterinarian-approved triage protocols to help assess urgency and determine appropriate next steps.

How to access Animal HealthLink:
  1. Call your regular veterinary clinic.
  2. Ask whether they use Animal HealthLink for after-hours telephone support.
  3. If they participate, follow their instructions to reach the triage team.
  4. Follow the guidance provided about contacting or travelling to veterinary care.

Important: Animal HealthLink is not a universal public emergency hotline. Access depends on your veterinary practice's participation. Do not delay emergency veterinary care while trying to access this service.
